Blitzfingers — guide

The board

The board holds two kinds of sound, one section each. One-shots (top) fire and finish — a kick, a clap, a word — and several can sound at once, so you can drum them. Loops (bottom) run until you stop them. Tap a pad to play it; tap a playing loop again to stop it. The small line under each section's name says how it behaves.

Stop all appears in the header while anything is sounding and silences everything at once — loops, one-shots, and a loop still fading out. It cuts, it does not fade.

Loops take turns

Only one loop plays at a time. Start another and the two crossfade: the old one fades out while the new one fades in, so a set never collides with itself. The fade takes two seconds unless you change it (Sound & levels); the loops' line on the board shows the time.

A loop set to keep playing steps out of that: it stays on when you start another one, so you can layer a beat, a bass line and a pad. It stops when you tap it again, or with Stop all. The pad shows a pin.

Loops start on the beat

When a loop recorded to the metronome is playing, a loop you tap does not start that instant — it waits for the next bar and comes in exactly there. Tap a little late or a little early and it still lands in time: no flam, and no loop drifting a fraction behind the other for as long as they play.

  • Only a known tempo is waited for. A take recorded to the metronome knows its tempo, so the wait is never longer than one bar. An imported loop has no known tempo: a loop you tap then starts at once, as a plain crossfade.
  • While it waits its pad pulses (VoiceOver says so, too). Tap it again to cancel; Stop all and switching banks cancel as well.
  • Switching crossfades on that bar; keep playing loops layer on it.
  • Nothing playing? A loop starts at once. Stopping is always immediate — and if you stop the loop the others were lining up to, a waiting one starts at once.
  • Speed changes are followed: the bar moves with the loop.

Hold and restart

A one-shot can be set to hold to repeat: it repeats while your finger is down and stops when you let go — the pad shows a finger. Or to restart on tap: a new tap stops the one still sounding and starts it over, so a longer sound — a word, a jingle — never piles up on itself. The pad shows ⏮. Both, and keep playing, are in the pad's Playback settings.

Performance Mode

Turn the phone sideways and the board goes edge to edge: no header, nothing but pads. Turning into it ends edit mode and closes any open screen, so the pads play; a recording or an overdub in progress stays open. Turn back to leave it.

Banks

A bank is one board — sixteen one-shots and sixteen loops. Use banks to keep sets apart: a drum kit, a party set, one board per song or show. The bank name in the header opens the list: tap a bank to switch to it, + New bank to add one, and the pencil beside a bank to rename or delete it.

Switching banks keeps a playing loop running, so you can change the pads under a groove without a gap. Sounds belong to their bank; a MIDI mapping does not — it belongs to a grid position, so the same controller pad plays position 3 in whichever bank is open.

To back up or share a bank, see Sound & levels ▸ Bank backup.

Editing a pad

The pencil in the header turns on edit mode: the pads lift and wobble, and the pencil becomes a check to finish. While editing, pads do not play — tap one to open its settings instead.

Arranging

Drag a pad to move it. Dropped onto another pad, it slots in there and the pads after it make room; dropped into an empty slot, it goes there and nothing else moves. On a touch screen, hold the pad a moment until it lifts, then drag — moving straight away scrolls the board instead, so you can scroll in edit mode without rearranging anything.

Drag a pad into the other section to move it between one-shots and loops. It works exactly like arranging: the pads there make room while you hold it over them, and an empty slot lights up to take it. A full section (16 pads) does not take it. Or use Move pad to … at the bottom of the pad's settings, which puts it in the first free slot. Whatever only makes sense in one section — hold, restart on tap, keep playing — is dropped, and the message that says so has an Undo.

The pad's settings

They open with the pad's name and colour, then lead into Playback, Trim, Effects, Shape & Transform, Overdub and MIDI. Everything you change is kept as you change it — there is nothing to save. Playback holds the pad's volume and its switches (hold, restart on tap, keep playing). Delete, for sounds you recorded or imported, asks first.

Colour

Auto gives the pad a colour derived from its name, so two neighbours never look alike; pick a swatch to choose one yourself. The colour sits in the pad's lower edge, so you find a pad without reading it.

Trim

Trim sets where a sound starts and ends without touching the file — you can always go back. Auto-trim cuts the quiet start and end; Full length keeps all of it; or drag the two handles on the waveform yourself.

For a precise cut, zoom in: pinch the waveform (or use + / − and Fit), drag the wave with one finger to move along it, and the strip underneath shows where you are in the whole sound. A handle dragged past the edge takes the view with it.

Beside Play you read the cut and its length to the hundredth of a second. Switch on Loop and the preview repeats the cut until you pause it — move a handle while it plays and you hear the new cut on the next pass. The recording screen works the same way.

A loop repeats exactly the trimmed part, so trimming is also how you make a loop line up.

Effects

Each pad has its own effects: Filter, Delay, Reverb, Chorus, Phaser, Distortion, Lo-fi, Pan and Bitcrush. Switch one on and its controls appear underneath. Changes are heard while the pad plays — play it from the Effects screen and dial them in by ear. Reset effects turns them all off again.

Filter is one knob: left of centre muffles (a low-pass sweeping down), right of centre thins (a high-pass sweeping up), and the middle is off. A pad with effects on shows FX.

Speed, pitch and reverse

Shape & Transform changes the sound itself rather than adding to it.

  • Speed plays faster or slower, and the pitch follows — like a record. Normal speed sits in the middle of the control, an octave down at a quarter, an octave up at three quarters.
  • Pitch shifts by semitones and keeps the tempo — up to an octave either way.
  • Reverse plays the sound backwards.

They combine: a sound can be sped up, pitched back down and played backwards at once.

Recording and importing

Tap an empty slot (the +) to add a sound there. Record… uses the microphone; Choose file… imports an audio file; Add from library… picks from the sounds that come with the app and from your own recordings in other banks.

A recording lands on the slot you tapped, keeps the date it was made, and can be trimmed, renamed and coloured like any other pad. New takes are numbered — Recording, Recording 2, Recording 3 — so they never share a name. Before saving you can trim the take on its waveform, and Normalize lifts a quiet take to a healthy level.

Recording to a metronome

Switch on the Metronome before you record, set the tempo (40–240 BPM) and the length in bars (1, 2, 4, 8 or 16). Record counts in one bar — big numbers on the screen, 4 3 2 1, then Go — records exactly the bars you set and stops by itself — cut to start on beat one. Every take at one tempo is the same length, so takes recorded one after another layer as loops, and they start on the beat. The pad's settings show the take's tempo under its name. Tempo and length are remembered for the next take.

With headphones you hear the click throughout. Without them the count-in clicks and taps, and during the take the beat only flashes — so nothing but you ends up in the recording.

Bluetooth headphones record through their own mic, which sounds like a phone call. For a clean take, use wired headphones, or the built-in mic.

Overdub

Overdub records a new pass over an existing sound and mixes the two — a harmony onto a loop, a second clap onto a clap. Open it from the pad's settings and tap Record: a big 4–3–2–1 counts you in, then the loop plays and records along with you each round, until you tap it again. Play just listens.

Headphones are required, wired or Bluetooth, and the app checks: without them the microphone would record the loop from the speaker along with you. The first Play or Record measures the round-trip delay of your headphones; Sync nudges it by ear if a layer lands early or late, and Recalibrate measures again after you switch headphones.

Undo and Redo step through the layers. Save as New keeps the original, Overwrite replaces it — and if the bank is full, the app offers the banks that still have room.

MIDI

Connect a controller by cable (a USB adapter) or over Bluetooth — Settings ▸ MIDI has the Bluetooth pairing — and Blitzfingers picks it up, also when you plug it in while the app runs. A pad's own settings lead to its mapping too.

Learning a control

In Settings ▸ MIDI, tap a pad on the small board to open it, then Learn on the row you want — Trigger, Volume, or one of the effect amounts — and move the control on your device. Learning again replaces the old assignment; the trash icon removes one. Beside the pads there are rows for Stop all and for the two section volumes. A mapped pad shows M.

Mappings belong to the grid position, not to the sound, so they survive switching banks. Set them up on your fullest bank.

Knobs, faders and endless encoders

Controllers report a knob in one of two ways, and each mapping remembers which — Blitzfingers finds out when you learn it, and shows it in the mapping's row:

  • Abs (absolute) — the control reports where it is, from one end of its travel to the other. Faders, and knobs with a start and an end.
  • Rel (relative) — the control reports that it moved, a step at a time. Endless encoders and jog wheels, which spin forever with no end stop.

If a mapping got it wrong — the value only flips between off and full, or runs backwards — tap Abs / Rel in its row to switch. ⇅ beside it turns a control upside down, for a fader that is louder at the bottom (a DJ controller's pitch fader, say).

First touch: Jump or Pick up

When a knob sits somewhere else than the value it controls, the first touch has to decide. Jump (the default) moves the value straight to the knob — always a reaction, sometimes an audible jump. Pick up waits until the knob reaches the value, then follows — no jump, and a short note tells you where to turn. Endless encoders never jump either way. Set it in Settings ▸ MIDI.

Keyboard

With a hardware keyboard — an iPad keyboard, or on a Mac — keys play pads. The number row fires one-shots 1–10 and ⇧ + 1–6 the rest; the letters Q to P and A to H play the sixteen loops. The keys follow the physical layout, so they sit in the same place on QWERTY and QWERTZ; each pad shows its key. A held key holds a hold-to-repeat pad. Escape is Stop all.

Sound & levels

Settings ▸ Sound sets the overall volume of the one-shots and of the loops, so you can balance the two sections for a room; the loop crossfade time (0 is a hard cut); and normalize loudness, which plays every sound at a similar level — the files stay as they are, so you can switch it off again. Each pad also has its own volume, in its Playback settings.

Bank backup

Settings ▸ Bank backup exports the bank you are in as one file — its sounds, every pad's settings, the layout and the dates the takes were made — to keep, or to send to someone. Import adds a saved bank next to your others; nothing is overwritten. MIDI mappings and app settings are not part of a bank and stay as they are.

Appearance

Settings ▸ Appearance hides what you no longer need once you know your board. On the pads: the names, and the symbols — MIDI marks, keyboard shortcuts, and the playback and effect symbols (hold, restart, keep playing, FX, reverse), each on its own switch under one Symbols switch that hides them all at once. On the board: the section explanations. Everything is shown by default, and names come back while you are editing — you cannot rename what you cannot identify.

When a small pad has more symbols than fit, the last ones fold into +2 (or however many) — the settings are all still there. Turn an effect knob on your controller and FX blinks — or the +2, when FX is folded into it.

Trying and buying

Blitzfingers is free to download, and for 14 days everything is yours — nothing is held back while you try it. The first time you open it, it says so and asks you to start the trial; the top row of Settings always shows how many days are left.

When the 14 days are up, your board keeps playing exactly as you built it: pads, loops, banks, the keyboard and MIDI still work, and so do the section volumes and exporting a bank. What stops is changing it — editing, adding or recording sounds, banks, importing a board, MIDI Learn, Appearance — until you unlock Blitzfingers once, for 14,99 €. The trial never turns into a charge by itself, and there is no subscription.

A purchase belongs to your Apple ID and usually comes back by itself on a new device. If it does not, use Restore purchase — in Settings, at the top.
