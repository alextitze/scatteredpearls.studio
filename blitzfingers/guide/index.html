Quick start

Five minutes from opening the app to a groove of your own.

  1. Tap a one-shot in the top section. It plays at once, and you can tap the next while it still sounds — drum on them.
  2. Tap a loop in the bottom section. It runs until you stop it. Tap another loop and the two crossfade; tap the playing one to stop it.
  3. Stop everything with the square that appears in the header while something sounds (on a keyboard: Space or Esc).
  4. Record your own: tap an empty slot (+), choose Record…, switch on the Metronome, and tap Start. A big 4–3–2–1 counts you in; the take stops by itself and lands on the slot.
  5. Shape it: the pencil in the header turns on edit mode — tap the new pad to trim it, name it, colour it and add effects.

Everything you change is kept as you change it — there is no Save. Your sounds stay on your device.

The board

The board holds two kinds of sound, one section each. One-shots (top) fire and finish — a kick, a clap, a word — and several can sound at once, so you can drum them. Loops (bottom) run until you stop them. Tap a pad to play it; tap a playing loop again to stop it. The small line under each section's name says how it behaves.

Stop all appears in the header while anything is sounding and silences everything at once — loops, one-shots, and a loop still fading out. It cuts, it does not fade.

The marks on a pad

Small marks on a pad tell you how it behaves without opening it. They sit in a row along the bottom; the keyboard key sits in the corner on the right.

Loops take turns

Only one loop plays at a time. Start another and the two crossfade: the old one fades out while the new one fades in, so a set never collides with itself. The fade takes two seconds unless you change it (Sound & levels); the loops' line on the board shows the time.

A loop set to keep playing steps out of that: it stays on when you start another one, so you can layer a beat, a bass line and a pad. It stops when you tap it again, or with Stop all. The pad shows a pin.

Loops start on the beat

When a loop recorded to the metronome is playing, a loop you tap does not start that instant — it waits for the next bar and comes in exactly there. Tap a little late or a little early and it still lands in time: no flam, and no loop drifting a fraction behind the other for as long as they play.

  • Only a known tempo is waited for. A take recorded to the metronome knows its tempo, so the wait is never longer than one bar. An imported loop has no known tempo: a loop you tap then starts at once, as a plain crossfade.
  • While it waits its pad pulses (VoiceOver says so, too). Tap it again to cancel; Stop all and switching banks cancel as well.
  • Switching crossfades on that bar; keep playing loops layer on it.
  • Nothing playing? A loop starts at once. Stopping is always immediate — and if you stop the loop the others were lining up to, a waiting one starts at once.
  • Speed changes are followed: the bar moves with the loop.

Hold and restart

A one-shot can be set to hold to repeat: it repeats while your finger is down and stops when you let go — the pad shows a finger. Or to restart on tap: a new tap stops the one still sounding and starts it over, so a longer sound — a word, a jingle — never piles up on itself. The pad shows ⏮. Both, and keep playing, are in the pad's Playback settings.

Performance Mode

Turn the phone sideways and the board goes edge to edge: no header, nothing but pads. Turning into it ends edit mode and closes any open screen, so the pads play; a recording or an overdub in progress stays open. Turn back to leave it.

Banks

A bank is one board: up to sixteen one-shots and sixteen loops. Make as many as you like and keep sets apart — a drum kit, a party set, one board per song or per show.

  • Switch: tap the bank name in the header and pick a bank from the list.
  • Add: + New bank in that list. It starts empty, ready for your sounds.
  • Rename or delete: the pencil beside a bank. Deleting asks first, and takes the bank's sounds with it. Your last bank stays — the board is never empty.

Blitzfingers comes with two banks ready to play: Groove — a beat, three basslines, two pads and a scale to play on top, all at one tempo and in one key — and Party. They are ordinary banks: change them, rearrange them, delete them. Deleting one is safe: Settings → Bank backup → Restore bundled banks brings back the missing ones as they shipped, and leaves the others alone. To undo your changes to one, open its pencil and choose Reset to original. Sounds you added to a bundled bank go with it when you delete or reset it.

Switching keeps the music going. A playing loop runs on while you change banks, so you can swap the pads under a groove without a gap; loops waiting for the next bar are cancelled.

Sounds belong to their bank. MIDI mappings and keyboard keys belong to the grid position, so the same control plays position 3 in whichever bank is open. To back up or share a bank, see Sound & levels ▸ Bank backup.

Editing a pad

The pencil in the header turns on edit mode: the pads wobble, the marks make room for the names, and the pencil becomes a check to finish. While editing, pads do not play — a tap opens their settings instead — and a stray MIDI hit does not play either. What is already playing keeps playing: rearrange, recolour or switch an effect on while the loops run, and you hear the change at once.

Arranging

  • Move a pad: drag it. Dropped onto another pad, it slots in there and the pads after it make room; dropped into an empty slot, it goes there and nothing else moves.
  • On a touch screen, hold the pad a moment until it lifts, then drag. Moving straight away scrolls the board instead — so you can scroll in edit mode without rearranging anything.
  • To the other section: drag it across, between one-shots and loops. The pads there make room while you hold it over them, and an empty slot lights up to take it; a full section (16 pads) does not. Or use Move pad to … at the bottom of the pad's settings, which puts it in the first free slot.
  • What only makes sense in one section — hold, restart on tap, keep playing — is dropped on the way, and the message that says so has an Undo.

The pad's settings

They open with the pad's name and colour; under the name, where the sound came from — "Recorded on 1 Oct 2026, 14:20 · 120 BPM · 4 bars". Below, one row per area:

  • Playback — the pad's volume, and its switches: hold to repeat and restart on tap for one-shots, keep playing for loops.
  • Trim — where it starts and ends.
  • Effects — the rack, with what is on in the row's badge.
  • Shape & Transform — speed, pitch and reverse.
  • Overdub — record layers over it (headphones).
  • MIDI — its controller mapping.

Everything you change is kept as you change it — there is nothing to save, and closing the screen is enough. Delete, for sounds you recorded or imported, asks first.

Colour

Auto gives the pad a colour derived from its name, so two neighbours rarely look alike; pick a swatch to choose one yourself. The colour sits in the pad's lower edge and fills it while it plays, so you find a pad without reading it.

Trim

Trim sets where a sound starts and ends without touching the file — you can always go back. Auto-trim cuts the quiet start and end; Full length keeps all of it; or drag the two handles on the waveform yourself.

For a precise cut, zoom in: pinch the waveform (or use + / − and Fit), drag the wave with one finger to move along it, and the strip underneath shows where you are in the whole sound. A handle dragged past the edge takes the view with it.

Beside Play you read the cut and its length to the hundredth of a second. Switch on Loop and the preview repeats the cut until you pause it — move a handle while it plays and you hear the new cut on the next pass. The recording screen works the same way.

A loop repeats exactly the trimmed part, so trimming is also how you make a loop line up.

Effects

Each pad has its own effect rack. Switch an effect on and its controls appear underneath; changes are heard while the pad plays, so play it from the Effects screen and dial it in by ear. A pad with effects on shows FX; the Effects row in its settings lists which. Reset effects turns them all off again.

Filter — one knob, two directions

The Sweep knob reads "LP" or "HP" with how far it is turned. It is the DJ trick: sweep a loop down to a muffle and back up for a drop.

The rest of the rack

  • Delay — echoes. Time between echoes, Feedback for how many, Mix for how loud.
  • Reverb — a space around the sound: Room, Hall or Plate, and Mix.
  • Chorus — thickens and shimmers: Rate, Intensity, Mix.
  • Phaser — a sweeping swoosh: Rate, Intensity, Mix.
  • Distortion — grit and drive, Smooth or Fuzz: Drive, Mix.
  • Lo-fi — an old tape: Wobble (warble), Interference (a dull, crackly cassette), Broken (dropouts) and Mix.
  • Pan — where it sits, left to right: Manual with a Position, or Auto, swinging side to side at a Rate and Intensity.
  • Bitcrush — crushed, digital: fewer Bits, a lower sample Rate, and Mix.

Every effect's main amount can sit on a knob of your controller — see Learning a control. Turning that knob switches the effect on, and FX blinks while it turns.

Speed, pitch and reverse

Shape & Transform changes the sound itself rather than adding to it.

Speed

Speed plays faster or slower, and the pitch follows — a sped-up voice gets high and quick. A loop played faster also gets shorter, and loops on the bar follow its new length.

Pitch

Pitch shifts by semitones — up to an octave either way — and keeps the tempo: a loop pitched up stays exactly as long. Use it to fit a loop to a song's key.

Reverse

Reverse plays the sound backwards — a cymbal that swells instead of fading, a word turned into a rising swoosh. The pad shows ←.

They combine: a sound can be sped up, pitched back down and played backwards at once. Speed and Pitch can sit on controller knobs too.

Recording and importing

Tap an empty slot — the + — to add a sound there. Record… uses the microphone; Choose file… imports an audio file; Add from library… picks from every sound of the banks that come with the app — with its settings, so a loop from Groove still starts on the bar in another bank — and from your own recordings in other banks. Whatever you add lands on the slot you tapped, in that section.

A recording, step by step

  1. Listening. The screen opens with the microphone live — the waveform moves with what it hears, so you can check the level before you start. Anything playing on the board stops first, so it does not end up in your take.
  2. Start recording, play or sing, and Stop recording. A timer runs while you record. (With the metronome on, the take stops by itself — see below.)
  3. Check the take. Play it, switch on Loop to hear it repeat, and set the cut: Auto-trim cuts the quiet start and end, Full length keeps all of it, or drag the handles yourself — exactly as in Trim. Normalize (on by default) lifts the take to a good, even level; the preview already plays it that way.
  4. Name it and Save. New takes are numbered — Recording, Recording 2, Recording 3 — so they never share a name; type over it if you like, and Return saves. Discard throws the take away and goes back to listening.

The trim is a setting, not a cut in the file — you can change it later in the pad's Trim. The pad remembers when it was recorded; its settings show the date.

Recording to a metronome

For loops that fit together, switch on the Metronome before you start, set the tempo with − and + (40–240 BPM; hold a button to run) and the length: 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 bars. Then Start:

  • It is exact. The take is cut to exactly the bars you set, starting on beat one — so every take at one tempo has the same length, to the sample. Takes recorded one after another layer as loops, and they start on the beat together.
  • It is kept on time. Headphones add a delay between the click and your ear, and the microphone adds one too. Blitzfingers measures it on the first take ("Measuring latency…") and cuts the take where you really played, not where the click was sent.
  • The screen shows where you are: "Count-in", then "Bar 1 of 4", with a dot per beat. Stop during a take cancels it — nothing half-finished is kept.
  • It remembers. Metronome on or off, the tempo and the length stay set for the next take. The pad's settings show the tempo under its name — "120 BPM · 4 bars".
  • A metronome take opens with Full length selected — its length is the point. You can still trim it, but then it no longer lines up bar for bar.

With headphones, without, and over Bluetooth

  • With headphones you hear the click on every beat — accented on beat one — through the count-in and the take.
  • Without headphones the count-in clicks and the phone taps, and during the take the beat only flashes. The microphone would otherwise hear the click (or the vibration), and it would end up in your recording.
  • Unplugging headphones during a take stops it ("Headphones unplugged — take stopped").
  • Bluetooth headphones work, with one difference: Blitzfingers records from the built-in microphone, and the headphones stay just for listening. A headset's own microphone records in phone-call quality and adds a delay its device under-reports.

The first time, the system asks for the microphone. If you said no, allow it again in Settings ▸ Blitzfingers ▸ Microphone.

Overdub

Overdub records new passes over a sound while it loops, and mixes them in — a harmony onto a loop, a second clap onto a clap, a beat built up round by round, like a loop pedal. Open it from the pad's settings: Overdub.

Recording a layer

  1. Put on headphones — wired or Bluetooth. Without them Overdub is greyed out ("Headphones required"): the microphone would record the loop from the speaker along with you.
  2. Wait a moment. The screen opens the microphone and measures the delay of your headphones by itself; Play shows "Measuring…" meanwhile.
  3. Tap Record. A big 4 3 2 1 counts you in, and on Go the loop starts and records along with you. Each time round, what you play is added to the loop — you hear your last round while you play the next.
  4. Tap Record again to stop recording. The loop plays on, so you can listen — and tap Record once more for another layer, straight away, without a count-in.
  • Play / Pause just listens. Pause holds the loop where it is, and Play goes on from there; pausing also stops recording.
  • The count-in follows the loop's tempo when it was recorded to the metronome; otherwise it counts at 120 BPM.
  • The loop is the pad's trimmed part. Your layers come in a little quieter than a fresh recording, so they sit with the loop rather than on top of it.
  • The red Recording button pulses while it records.

Undo, Redo and Clear

  • Undo takes back the last round you recorded; Redo brings it back.
  • Clear takes the loop back to the original sound, all layers gone. It is available as soon as anything was overdubbed.

Timing & sync

The measured delay is what keeps your layers in time. If one lands a hair early or late anyway, open Timing & sync: the Sync slider moves the layers up to 80 ms either way, by ear. Recalibrate measures again — after you switch headphones, say. The status line says what was measured ("Calibrated · 42 ms round-trip"). Bluetooth headphones leak no sound to measure, so there the delay the system reports is used, and Sync is your fine-tuning.

Keeping it

  • Save as New keeps the original and adds the overdub as a new pad — "Beat (dub)" — in the same section. If the bank is full, Blitzfingers offers the banks that still have room, or a new bank; you stay where you are.
  • Overwrite — only for sounds you recorded or imported — replaces the original, after asking.
  • Discard leaves without keeping anything. Unplugging the headphones closes Overdub too.

MIDI

A MIDI controller turns Blitzfingers into an instrument with real buttons: pads you hit, faders for the section volumes, knobs for the effects. Any class-compliant controller works — over a cable (a USB adapter where needed) or Bluetooth.

Connecting a controller

  • By cable: plug it in. Blitzfingers finds it by itself, also while the app is running.
  • Over Bluetooth: Settings ▸ MIDI ▸ the Bluetooth button at the top opens the system's pairing screen; pick your device there.
  • The line under the title says what is connected — "Connected: DJ2GO2", or "No MIDI device found". Press anything on the controller and it shows what arrived ("last: noteon …"), so you can see that the device talks.

Learning a control

You do not type note numbers — you show Blitzfingers which control you mean:

  1. In Settings ▸ MIDI, tap a pad on the small board. Its mapping card opens. (A pad's own settings lead there too, under MIDI.)
  2. Tap Learn on the row you want. It pulses, and the status line says "Listening… press a control".
  3. Hit the pad or move the knob on your controller. The row shows what it learned — "Note 36", "CC 7". Learn keeps listening: move a different control and that one replaces it. Tap Learn again to finish.

What can be learned

  • Trigger — a pad or key on the controller plays the pad. A one-shot fires; a loop starts or stops; a hold-to-repeat pad plays while the key is down. Triggers learn from pads and keys (notes); how hard you hit does not matter.
  • Volume — a knob or fader sets the pad's volume.
  • Effects — one knob per effect for its amount: Filter, Delay, Reverb, Chorus, Phaser, Distortion, Lo-fi and Bitcrush, Pan, and also Speed and Pitch. Turning a mapped effect knob switches the effect on.
  • Global — Stop all (a pad or key), and the one-shots and loops volume (knobs or faders).

The trash icon removes a mapping, after asking. A mapped pad shows M (Settings ▸ Appearance can hide it); on the small MIDI board, dots show what each pad has mapped.

Mappings belong to the grid position, not to the sound: the controller's third pad plays position 3 in whichever bank is open. Set them up once, on your fullest bank — they work in all of them.

Knobs, faders and endless encoders: Abs and Rel

Controllers send a knob in one of two ways, and each mapping remembers which:

  • Abs (absolute) — the control reports where it is, from one end of its travel to the other. Faders and knobs with an end stop.
  • Rel (relative) — the control reports that it moved, one step at a time. Endless encoders and jog wheels, which spin forever. Each step moves the value by a fine amount, so a full turn is a sweep, not a leap.

You do not have to know which you have: Learn tells them apart by how the control moves. If it guessed wrong — the value only flips between off and full, or does not move — tap Abs / Rel in the row to switch.

Upside down: ⇅

The ⇅ button in a knob's row turns the control around: up becomes down. For a fader that is built the other way — a DJ controller's pitch fader, louder towards you — or a knob you would rather turn left to open.

First touch: Jump or Pick up

A knob on your controller and the value it controls can disagree — you changed the volume on the screen, or switched banks. The first touch has to decide what happens:

  • Jump (the default) — the value goes straight to where the knob is. You always get a reaction, sometimes an audible step.
  • Pick up — the value waits until the knob reaches it, then follows. Nothing jumps; a short note tells you which way to turn.
  • Endless encoders (Rel) never jump either way — they only ever move the value from where it is.

Choose it in Settings ▸ MIDI ▸ First touch; it applies to every mapping.

While you edit, and after the trial

In edit mode the pads ignore MIDI triggers, so a stray hit does not play while you arrange; Stop all and the section volumes keep working. After the trial, mappings keep playing — only learning and changing them waits for the unlock.

Keyboard

With a hardware keyboard — an iPad keyboard, or on a Mac — keys play pads. The number row fires one-shots 1–10 and ⇧ + 1–6 the rest; the letters Q to P and A to H play the sixteen loops. The keys follow the physical layout, so they sit in the same place on QWERTY and QWERTZ; each pad shows its key. A held key holds a hold-to-repeat pad. Space or Escape is Stop all — Space is the one to use on an iPad keyboard, which has no Escape key. (When a button on a screen has the focus, Space presses that button instead.)

The keycaps on the pads appear once a key has been pressed, so they only show on a device that has a keyboard; Settings ▸ Appearance can hide them.

Sound & levels

Settings ▸ Sound is what you reach for in a new room:

  • Overall one-shots volume and overall loops volume — balance the two sections against each other: loops under the hits, or the other way round.
  • Loop crossfade / fade — how long loops take to cross over when you switch, 0 to 5 seconds; 0 is a hard cut. The loops' line on the board shows the time.
  • Normalize loudness — plays every sound at a similar level, so a quiet recording and a loud sample sit together. The files stay as they are; switch it off to hear them as recorded. With it off, a pad below full volume shows a thin bar at its top.

Each pad also has its own volume, in its Playback settings. The section volumes stay open after the trial — balancing a room is playing, not building.

Bank backup

Settings ▸ Bank backup exports the bank you are in as one file — its sounds, every pad's settings, the layout and the dates the takes were made. Keep it, or send it to someone with Blitzfingers. Import adds a saved bank next to your others; nothing is overwritten. MIDI mappings and app settings are not part of a bank and stay as they are. Exporting always works, also after the trial — your sounds are yours.

Appearance

Settings ▸ Appearance hides what you no longer need once you know your board by heart. A pad behaves exactly the same; only what it shows changes. Everything is shown by default.

  • On the pads — Names. Without them the board reads by colour and position. Names come back while you edit (you cannot rename what you cannot identify), and screen readers always announce them.
  • On the pads — Symbols, one switch for all marks, or each on its own: MIDI, Keyboard shortcuts (on a device with a keyboard), and Playback & effects — hold, restart, keep playing, FX, reverse.
  • On the board — Category explanations, the small line under "One-shots" and "Loops".

When a small pad has more marks than fit, the last ones fold into +2 (or however many) — the settings are all still there. Turn an effect knob on your controller and FX blinks — or the +2, when FX is folded into it.

Trying and buying

Blitzfingers is free to download, and for 14 days everything is yours — nothing is held back while you try it. The first time you open it, it says so and asks you to start the trial; the top row of Settings always shows how many days are left.

When the 14 days are up, your board keeps playing exactly as you built it: pads, loops, banks, the keyboard and MIDI still work, and so do the section volumes and exporting a bank. What stops is changing it — editing, adding or recording sounds, banks, importing a board, MIDI Learn, Appearance — until you unlock Blitzfingers once, for 14,99 €. The trial never turns into a charge by itself, and there is no subscription.

A purchase belongs to your Apple ID and usually comes back by itself on a new device. If it does not, use Restore purchase — in Settings, at the top.

When something does not work

No sound

  • Turn the device up with its volume buttons. Blitzfingers plays even with the ringer switch on silent.
  • Check the levels: Settings ▸ Sound has one volume for the one-shots and one for the loops, and each pad has its own in its Playback settings.
  • With headphones or a speaker connected, the sound goes there — check where Control Centre sends it.

The sound comes late

  • Bluetooth headphones and speakers add a delay of their own, often a fifth of a second or more. For playing live, wired headphones or the built-in speaker answer at once.
  • If every tap suddenly sounds late, close Blitzfingers completely (swipe it away in the app switcher) and open it again.

A Bluetooth MIDI controller does not show up

  • A Bluetooth MIDI device connects to one host at a time. If it is still connected to another phone, tablet or computer, it does not appear — disconnect it there, or switch that device's Bluetooth off for a moment.
  • Blitzfingers needs Bluetooth permission: Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Bluetooth.
  • On a device with Screen Time limits, Bluetooth Sharing must be allowed.

Overdub is greyed out

Overdub needs headphones, so the microphone does not record the loop from the speaker. Connect wired or Bluetooth headphones and it becomes available.

A purchase is missing on a new device

Settings ▸ the top row ▸ Restore purchase. It needs the same Apple ID as the purchase.
