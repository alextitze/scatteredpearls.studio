The board

The board holds two kinds of sound. One-shots fire and finish — a kick, a clap, a word — and several can overlap. Loops run until you stop them, and starting a second loop crossfades out of the first, so a set never collides with itself.

Loops start on the beat

When a loop recorded to the metronome is playing, a loop you tap does not start that instant — it waits for the next beat and comes in exactly there. Tap a little late or a little early and it still lands in time: no flam, no loop drifting a fraction behind the other one for as long as they play.

  • Which beat: a take recorded to the metronome knows its tempo, so the next loop waits for the next bar — never longer. An imported loop has no known tempo: then a loop you tap starts at once, as a plain crossfade.
  • While it waits its pad pulses (VoiceOver says so, too). Tap it again to cancel; Stop all and switching banks cancel too.
  • Switching from one loop to another crossfades on that beat as well, so the groove does not stumble. Keep playing loops layer on it.
  • Nothing playing? A loop starts at once. Stopping is always immediate — and if you stop the loop the others were lining up to, a waiting one starts at once.
  • Speed changes are followed: the beat moves with the loop.

Tap a pad to play it. Stop all appears in the header while anything is sounding, and the Escape key does the same on a keyboard.

Turn the phone and the board goes edge to edge — Performance Mode: no header, nothing but pads. Turning into it ends edit mode and closes any open screen, so the pads play; a recording or an overdub in progress stays open.

Hold, restart and keep playing

A one-shot can be set to hold to repeat: it repeats while your finger is down and stops when you let go — the pad shows a finger. Or to restart on tap: a new tap stops the one still playing and starts it over, so a longer sound never piles up on itself — the pad shows ⏮.

A loop can be set to keep playing: it stays on when you start another loop instead of crossfading out, and stops when you tap it again — the pad shows a pin. All three live in the pad's Playback settings.

Banks

A bank is one board. Use them to keep sets apart — a drum kit, a party set, a show. The bank name in the header opens the list; you can rename, add and delete there.

Each bank holds up to 16 one-shots and 16 loops. Sounds are not shared between banks, but a MIDI mapping is: it belongs to a grid position, so the same controller pad plays position 3 in whichever bank is open.

Editing a pad

The pencil in the header turns on edit mode — the pads lift and wobble. Tap one to open its settings; drag one to reorder — onto another pad to slot it in there, into an empty slot to move it there without shifting anything else; tap an empty slot to add a sound there. On a touch screen, hold the pad a moment until it lifts, then drag: moving straight away scrolls the board instead, so you can scroll in edit mode without rearranging anything.

Drag a pad into the other section to move it between one-shots and loops — it works just like reordering: the pads there make room while you hold it over them, and an empty slot lights up to take it. Or use Move to … in its settings. A full section (16 pads) does not take it. Whatever only makes sense in one of them (hold, restart on tap, keep playing) is dropped, and the message that says so has an Undo.

A pad's settings screen holds its name and colour, and leads into Playback, Trim, Effects, Shape & Transform, Overdub and MIDI. Everything you change is kept as you change it — there is nothing to save. Delete is the exception and asks first.

Colour

Auto gives the pad a colour derived from its name, so two neighbours never look alike; pick a swatch to override it.

Trim

Trim sets where a sound starts and ends without touching the file. Drag the two handles on the waveform, or use Auto to cut leading and trailing silence. Full length puts it back.

Beside Play you read the cut and its length, to the hundredth of a second. Switch on Loop and the preview repeats the cut until you pause it — move a handle while it plays and you hear the new cut on the next pass. The recording screen works the same way.

A loop repeats the trimmed region, so trimming is also how you make a loop line up.

Effects

Each pad has its own effects: Filter, Delay, Reverb, Chorus, Phaser, Distortion, Lo-fi, Pan and Bitcrush. Switch one on and its controls appear underneath. Changes are audible while the pad plays, so you can dial them in by ear.

Filter is one knob: left of centre muffles (a low-pass sweeping down), right of centre thins (a high-pass sweeping up), and the middle is off.

Speed, pitch and reverse

Shape & Transform changes the sound itself rather than adding to it.

  • Speed plays faster or slower, and the pitch follows — like a record. Normal speed sits in the middle of the control, an octave down at a quarter, an octave up at three quarters.
  • Pitch shifts by semitones and keeps the tempo. Up to an octave either way.
  • Reverse plays the sound backwards.

Recording and importing

Tap an empty slot to add a sound there. You can record with the microphone, import an audio file, or pick one from the built-in library.

A recording lands on the slot you tapped, with its date kept, and can be trimmed, renamed and coloured like any other pad. New takes are numbered — Recording, Recording 2, Recording 3 — so they never share a name.

Recording to a metronome

Switch on the Metronome before you record, set the tempo (40–240 BPM) and the length in bars (1, 2, 4, 8 or 16). Record counts in one bar, records exactly the bars you set and stops by itself — cut to start on beat one. Every take at one tempo is the same length, so takes recorded one after another layer as loops. Tempo and length are remembered for the next take.

With headphones you hear the click throughout. Without them the count-in clicks and taps, and during the take the beat only flashes — so nothing but you ends up in the recording.

Overdub

Overdub records a new pass over an existing sound and mixes the two — layering a harmony onto a loop, or a second clap onto a clap.

Headphones are required, and the app checks: without them the microphone would record the loop playing out of the speaker along with you. The first Start measures the round-trip delay of your headphones automatically; Sync nudges it by ear if a layer lands early or late, and Recalibrate re-measures after you switch headphones.

Undo and Redo step through the layers. Save it as a new sound or overwrite the original — and if the bank is full, the app offers the banks that still have room.

MIDI

Connect a controller by cable (a USB adapter) or over Bluetooth, and Blitzfingers shows it in Settings ▸ MIDI. You can also reach a single pad's mapping from that pad's own settings.

Learning a control

Tap a pad in the MIDI screen to open it, then Learn on the row you want — Trigger, Volume, or one of the effects — and move the control on your device. Learning again replaces the old assignment. The trash icon removes one.

Mappings belong to the grid position, not to the sound, so they survive switching banks. Set them up on your fullest bank.

Knob mode: Absolute or Relative

Controllers report their knobs in one of two ways, and Blitzfingers needs to know which.

  • Absolute — the control reports where it is, from one end of its travel to the other. Normal for faders and for knobs with a start and an end. This is the default.
  • Relative — the control reports that it moved, a step at a time. Used by endless encoders, which spin forever with no end stop.

If a knob spins endlessly and mapping a volume behaves oddly — jumping to one end, or only reacting at the extremes — switch to Relative. The setting is in Settings ▸ MIDI.

Some endless knobs still report absolute positions. If Relative makes things worse, switch back: your device is one of those.

Appearance

Settings ▸ Appearance hides what you no longer need once you know your board. On the pads: the names, and the symbols — MIDI marks, keyboard shortcuts, and the playback and effect symbols (hold, restart, keep playing, FX, reverse), each on its own switch under one Symbols switch that hides them all at once. On the board: the category explanations. Everything is shown by default, and names come back while you are editing — you cannot rename what you cannot identify.

When a small pad has more symbols than fit, the last ones fold into +2 (or however many) — the settings are all still there.
