Why

Why I made Blitzfingers

I wanted a soundboard I actually enjoyed. The App Store is full of them — but they drowned me in ads, or hid the good parts behind a subscription. That's not my thing. So I built the one I was missing.

The rule was simple: open it and just play. Tap a pad, a sound fires. Stack one-shots like a drummer; layer loops into a groove. Fun in the first second — no setup, no manual.

But I didn't want "easy" to mean "shallow." So it goes as deep as you like: record your own sounds, trim and shape them on a waveform, add real effects, bend the pitch, layer takes, and play the whole thing from a MIDI controller.

And the small things are there because I sweated them — colours that help your eye find a sound, haptics you feel on every hit, a screen that won't fall asleep mid-take, sound that steps aside politely when a call comes in.

Made with care, by one pair of hands. Pay once, and it's yours.

Play

Tap-to-play pads

A board of colourful pads — tap to fire a sound instantly. Pick the pad size that suits you: Auto, S, M or L.

One-shots that overlap

Percussive hits stack and overlap, so you can actually finger-drum instead of waiting for one sound to finish.

Loops with crossfade

Backing loops play one at a time and crossfade smoothly — or layer them to build up a bed.

Keep playing & hold-to-repeat

Latch a loop so it keeps running under the next one, or hold a pad to repeat it while pressed.

Banks

Group your sounds into named sets — create, rename and switch banks for different gigs, jokes or projects.

Keyboard & Stop-all

On a keyboard, fire one-shots from the number row and loops from letters. One key stops everything.

Make it yours

Record your own

Capture sounds with the mic and turn them into pads — your voice, an instrument, anything around you.

Trim & normalize

Auto-trim the quiet start and end on a waveform, or set it by hand. Even out levels so every pad sits right.

Effects rack Pro

Filter, delay, reverb, chorus, phaser, distortion, lo-fi, pan and bitcrush — tweaked live, with no clicks.

Pitch, speed & reverse Pro

Shift pitch ±12 semitones without changing tempo, speed a sound up or down, or play it backwards.

Overdub layering Pro

Sing or play along and stack takes on each loop pass, with automatic latency calibration. Headphones required.

Per-pad editing

Rename, recolour, set volume, and reorder pads by dragging. Your colour follows the sound.

MIDI controller Pro

Map pads to a MIDI controller, including Bluetooth MIDI, and learn notes and CCs for hands-on play.

Themes & jewel-tone colours

System, Light or Dark. Each sound keeps its own hue so your eye finds it fast.

Haptics & screen-awake

Feel each hit with subtle haptics, and the screen stays awake while you record or play.

FAQ

Good to know

What is Blitzfingers?

A lean soundboard and finger-drumming app for iPhone. You tap colourful pads to play loops and one-shots, and you can record and shape your own sounds too.

How much does it cost?

Blitzfingers is a small one-time purchase — no subscription. The advanced creative tools (effects, overdub, export and MIDI) are an additional one-time Pro unlock.

Is there a subscription?

No. Blitzfingers is pay-once. You unlock Pro with a single purchase and keep it — there is no subscription and no recurring fee.

Do I need an account?

No account, no sign-up. There is nothing to log into.

Does it collect my data?

No. There are no ads and no tracking. Your sounds and boards stay on your device — nothing is uploaded.

Which platforms does it run on?

iPhone first. macOS and Android are planned and will follow.

Can I use my own sounds?

Yes. Record with the mic or import your own audio, then trim, colour and arrange each pad. You can export a whole board and share or re-import it.

Does it work with a MIDI controller?

Yes — map pads to a MIDI controller, including Bluetooth MIDI, and learn notes and CCs. (Pro.)

My MIDI knob controls volume oddly — what are “Absolute” and “Relative”?

Under MIDI controller you can choose how knobs are read. Absolute suits most knobs and faders — the ones with a fixed start and end; the app follows the knob’s position directly. Pick Relative for endless encoders that spin without a start or end (they send only small step changes). So: if a knob mapped to volume jumps, sticks or barely moves, switch it to Relative; otherwise leave it on Absolute. Most controllers work on Absolute.
