The layout

Why loops and one-shots live apart

Two kinds of sound, two ways of behaving. Keeping them in separate halves of the board is what lets you play with both hands without thinking about it.

One-shots

your hits — a clap, a stab, a vocal snip
  • Kick
  • Clap
  • Zap
  • Hat
  • Tap it and it fires instantly.
  • They overlap and stack — three at once is normal, which is what makes finger-drumming possible. A longer one can restart on each tap instead.
  • Hold one to make it repeat while your finger stays down. That is the finger above.

Loops

your bed — a groove, a drone, a backing
  • Bass
  • Drone
  • Chord
  • Air
  • Tap it and it runs, and keeps running.
  • One at a time — tap another and it crossfades in, so the vibe never jars.
  • Set one to keep playing and it stays on under the next. That is the pin above.
  • Recorded to the metronome, the next loop waits for the bar — tap a little late and it still lands in time.

Picture it: a warm loop holds the groove while you tap claps and zaps over the top. That is the whole app, and you had to learn nothing to get there.

Features

Make it yours

The board that ships is a starting point. Everything on it can be replaced, renamed and rearranged until it is your board.

  • Record your own

    Capture anything with the mic and it becomes a pad — your voice, an instrument, the dog. Switch on the metronome and every take at one tempo comes out the same length, ready to layer.

  • Trim and normalize

    Cut the silence off the start and end on a waveform, or set it by hand — with the cut looping while you listen. Even out levels so one pad does not shout.

  • Name it, colour it, move it

    Drag a pad where your hand expects it — into a gap, between two others, or across from the one-shots to the loops. Give it a name and a hue — the colour sits in the pad's lip, so you find it without reading.

  • Banks

    Group sounds into named sets and switch between them — one for the gig, one for the running joke.

  • Keyboard, and one key for silence

    On a keyboard the number row fires one-shots and letters fire loops. One key stops everything at once.

The deep end

When the board stops being enough

The parts you grow into. They are all in the app from the first day — nothing is held back, not even while you try it.

  • Effects rack

    Filter, delay, reverb, chorus, phaser, distortion, lo-fi, pan and bitcrush — per pad, live, without a click.

  • Pitch, speed and reverse

    Shift twelve semitones either way without changing tempo, stretch a sound out, or play it backwards.

  • Overdub

    Sing or play along and stack takes on each loop pass. Latency calibrates itself; bring headphones.

  • MIDI controller

    Map pads to a controller, over cable or Bluetooth, and put one "how much" knob on each effect.

  • Export and import boards

    A whole bank as one file you can see in Files and send to someone. It comes back intact.

Why

I built the one I was missing

I wanted a soundboard I actually enjoyed. The App Store is full of them — but they drowned me in ads, or rented me the good parts by the month. So I built this one: open it and just play, fun in a second, and still deep when you want more.

Nothing here renews. Nothing watches you. You buy it, and it is yours.

Made with care, by one pair of hands. — Alex

Everything stays on the device

Your sounds and boards live on your phone. No cloud, no account, nothing uploaded — and it all works with the plane mode on.

It behaves on iOS

Sound comes through even with the silent switch on, it steps aside for a call or Siri, and it stops when you unplug the headphones.

Small things you only miss when they are gone

A short haptic under every hit, and a screen that refuses to sleep while you are recording or playing.

Questions

Before you buy

Is there a subscription?

No. You buy the full version once, and you are never billed again.

What does the trial include?

Everything, for 14 days. There is no lite version — what you try is what you buy. The trial ends by itself; it never turns into a charge.

And after the 14 days?

Your board keeps playing exactly as you built it — only changing it waits for the unlock. Nothing you made is taken away, and exporting it always works.

Where do my sounds live?

On your device, and only there. No account, no upload, no cloud.

Does it work offline?

Entirely. The app never needs a connection.

iPhone, iPad or Mac?

All three, and one purchase covers them. Android is a maybe, not a promise.

Can I use my own sounds?

That is the point. Record them, import them, trim them, keep them.

Can I back up or share a board?

Yes. Export a bank as one file — its sounds, every pad's settings and the layout — and import it again on another iPhone, iPad or Mac, or send it to someone. Importing adds it next to your banks; nothing is overwritten. Exporting always works, also after the trial.

Can I get a refund?

Purchases go through Apple, so refunds do too — ask Apple through Report a Problem.
